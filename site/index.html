How this compares with other studies

Most published work on Indian founders counts the backgrounds of people who already won: unicorn and soonicorn founders. That tells you who is at the top, not what got them funded, because there is no group of founders who didn't make it to compare against. I couldn't find another India study with a comparison group.

Where India differs from the US

  • The MBA isn't a red flag here. US 0.60×. India about 1.2× overall, about 1× for generic MBAs. Indian VCs don't penalise it. They just don't pay much for it on its own.
  • Consulting counts for more, for now. Ex-McKinsey/BCG/Bain is about 2.5× over the full window, against 1.29× in the US. It weakened in 2025–26.
  • The insider edge is bigger. Ex tier-1 VC staff is the top signal in both countries: about 5× here, 3.0× there. Only 37 startups have it, so the range is wide.
  • Your address matters. A Bengaluru startup is about 1.9× likelier to get a tier-1 lead than an otherwise similar one elsewhere. Delhi NCR is 0.65×. Mumbai is flat.

What counts as tier-1

The 15 Indian venture funds that most often lead Seed and Series A rounds. Accelerators, angel networks and debt funds are not counted. The list isn't published, so the study stays about founders, not funds. My own employer isn't on it.

Method

  • Universe: every India-based company with a Seed or Series A round dated 1 Jan 2021 to 2 Nov 2026 in Tracxn: 15,224 rounds, 8,434 companies.
  • Kept: 6,990 companies where Tracxn describes at least one founder: 15,070 founders.
  • Outcome: at least one of those rounds had a tier-1 fund marked as lead investor (570 companies, 8.2%).
  • Signals: "any founder has X", coded from Tracxn's founder profiles (education, past employers) and its Team Background and Founder Highlights tags.
  • Model: one logit with all signals and controls. Nested signals (any MBA, any VC/PE, repeat founder, no elite credential) are swapped in one at a time. q-values are Benjamini–Hochberg. Signals held by fewer than 25 startups are dropped, as in Deedy's study.
  • Periods: the 2021–24 and 2025–26 runs use only rounds inside each window, for both the outcome and the controls.

Limits

  • Correlation, not causation. Credentials travel with networks, cities and sectors. The model controls for some of this, not all.
  • Recent data is thinner. 75% of 2025–26 startups have a lead investor recorded, against 84% for 2021–24. Some recent tier-1 leads are probably still missing.
  • Missing profiles count as "no". Founders whose education Tracxn doesn't record are treated as not having the signal, which biases effects toward 1×.
  • Coded from text. Signals are parsed from profile text with rules, then spot-checked by hand. Expect a few miscodes.
  • No gender field. Tracxn's company export has no founder gender, so this study can't test it. The women-founder funding numbers above show why that gap matters.
  • Robustness: results barely move when the outcome is "any tier-1 participation" or when the sample is limited to rounds with named leads.

Remarks

The paper that gets you in the room still matters in Indian venture. But it matters through who you've worked for and who you know. The degree doesn't do much on its own. Half of all funded startups here had no elite credential on the founding team. Only a quarter of the tier-1-led ones did. In the last two years, having built inside a unicorn has started to count for more than having advised one.
